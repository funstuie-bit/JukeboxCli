# JukeboxCli 1.6.1

A patch for YouTube videos that wouldn't play because picture and sound arrived
as separate streams. One release for Mac and Linux; music and settings are kept.

## What's fixed

- Separate YouTube video and audio streams now play through the same mpv process.
  Combined streams and silent videos remain supported. Playing doesn't save or
  merge media files on disk.
- Switching between audio and video keeps the queue, pause state, volume and
  position when seekable. Returning to audio clears the separate soundtrack.
- Cached links expire when either stream expires; a failed load refreshes the
  pair once before reporting the problem.
- Video errors explain format, cookie/source restrictions, unavailable videos
  and lookup timeouts instead of only suggesting audio. Signed URLs and raw
  extractor output aren't shown or saved in listening history.

Includes [1.6.0's video browsing, saved searches and mouse controls](https://github.com/funstuie-bit/JukeboxCli/releases/tag/v1.6.0).
See [video controls and limits](https://github.com/funstuie-bit/JukeboxCli/blob/v1.6.1/docs/video-playback.md).

## Update

```sh
brew update
brew upgrade jukeboxcli
jukeboxcli --version
```

Expect **1.6.1**, then quit and reopen JukeboxCli. Source installations can run
`./update.sh` from their checkout, retaining any custom prefix.

## Tested and remaining limits

On Apple Silicon macOS, 886 tests passed with 6 skipped. Typecheck, build/import
checks and the production redraw guard passed. Isolated, muted real-mpv checks
covered generated separate-stream files and a public YouTube video that failed
in 1.6.0: video/audio/video switching kept one player, queue and paused position;
playback advanced and the test player exited cleanly. No user profile was changed.

Mac and Linux installation workflows check the release source and independent
install/reinstall; Mac also installs and tests the pinned Homebrew formula.
These checks don't establish every terminal, Intel Mac or physical media-control
combination. YouTube availability/authentication still depends on the source;
conflicting picture/sound HTTP headers are refused explicitly. Longer memory/GPU
and broader live-source acceptance remain ongoing.
